How our meeting decks tell the story
Based on a comparison of 7 sales calls (Laverra, Ecoveld ×2, Intopia, D4Project, Boydens ×2, All Party Tents, Jakob) with the deck shown, November 2026. Finding: the calls were won on what Wannes said, not on the slides. Fewer than half the slides were shown; the moments that moved prospects (their own numbers, live calculations, sector stories, live proof, honest limits, price and cash-flow terms) were all improvised. Every Revenue Lab deck, sales or client, follows these rules:
1.  Start with their situation, in their words. Slide 2 is always "Wat we gehoord hebben" with their numbers and pains (Jakob's deck slide 2 was the best slide; Laverra engaged on "hot leads lost after the show", not on generic blind spots).
2.  Their numbers, calculated together on the slide. Leads → appointments → shows → deals → cost per deal, with blanks filled in live. Intopia came alive at "€2,000 ÷ 13 ≈ €153 per lead, ±€1,500 per deal"; Working From's improvised maths wobbled because it wasn't prepared.
3.  Frame the problem as a choice before the solution ("lower cost per lead, or 4× the volume?"). That question got Intopia's commitment.
4.  One step of reasoning per slide, his best line as the headline. "Een lead is niks waard als er geen afspraak uitkomt", "Een afspraak is niks waard als ze niet doorgaat", "Binnen zeven dagen weet je of het werkt". Not bare titles like "De revenue flow" or "Dashboarding".
5.  The why and the mechanism on the slide, not just the number: why the show rate rises (the flows), why close rates rise (no shared leads), how the form filters (timing question, Meta trained via API), shown as one simple diagram.
6.  Proof from their sector and size, checkable. Named Flemish references they know, with budget and starting point. Energyking's €12k/month scared Ecoveld; Dryplan, Flowplus at €600–900 and the live Energie De Kempen ad account turned it ("dan kunnen wij met minder starten").
7.  Live proof and something forwardable: an anonymised ad-account or dashboard screenshot (Belivair 847 → 312 → 36.8% was the clearest moment at All Party Tents), plus a one-pager the buyer can share (Ecoveld asked for screenshots without client names for the group).
8.  Their own material next to ours: their confirmation mail, ad or landing page vs our version, and three hooks built from their customers' questions. The Intopia mail audit and the Laverra hooks ("Ja, exact") were the most concrete moments, both improvised.
9.  Say what we don't promise: realistic lead quality (one figure: 30–40% qualified), B2B limits. "Ik zou liegen als ik zeg…" produced "ik heb vertrouwen". No "100% relevante leads" on slides or aloud.
10.  Week by week: day 1–7 test, day 30 live, day 31 leads, kill/keep after 7 days. That was Laverra's reason to pick us over two agencies.
11.  Scope and what we need from them: what they already have vs what we add (phases ticked per client), and what it asks of the owner (who's on camera, no job sites to film → AI editor).
12.  Price, terms and cash flow on a slide, identical to the Qwilr offer: included/excluded, minimum ad spend and why (€30/day), CRM, setup fee, commitment and exit, payment spread. Spoken prices differed from the slides (€1,850 / €2,150 vs €2,500) and terms differed per call; Boydens turned to "90% ja" only when month 1 was spread over 6 months.
13.  Objection slides in reserve: commission vs fee, why not Solvari/Bobex, actors and existing footage, "video is cringe", other markets, results timeline.
14.  Beyond ads: one business-model or upsell idea specific to them (Laverra's €10/month maintenance contract got the strongest reaction).
15.  Planned check-ins and a next step done in the meeting: "ligt dat in lijn?", "wat is het meest waardevolle?", "nog twijfels?", then book the next meeting and open the offer live.
16.  One number, one definition, everywhere. Fix the template's errors (show rate 60/64/74 → 85/95, "3×" vs "4×", Ecotech vs Recotex, €17 vs €27, Flowplus €1.8M vs €2M, empty phase 5, duplicate 30–60 days slide).
17.  Short core, appendix for the rest: ±15–20 story slides; generic template parts only where they fit (Jakob's deck repeated the lead-first pitch he had called "meer van hetzelfde").

Sector benchmarks
Starting values (Nov 2026), to be replaced by our own data per sector:
• Lead → appointment: 33–40% · appointment → deal: 27% · cost per lead: €15–30
• Closed revenue per month: €80–110k in airco, solar panels and similar; €200–250k in roofing and facades
How it becomes dynamic: per sector the agent calculates the toppers (top 25%), the average and the losers (bottom 25%) from our own client data: cost per lead and cost per appointment from Client Check (ad reports and Strilio leads), appointment → deal and closed revenue from Strilio, where clients track their deals there. Refreshed monthly, so the kick-off always shows current numbers. Example with the starting values: 10 deals → 37 appointments → 93–112 leads → €1.4–3.4k ad spend per month.
Open: appointment → deal and closed revenue only exist for clients that log deals in Strilio. Until that's enough data per sector, the starting values above are used.

CRM / go-live meeting (week 3, ±60 min)
Live in the client's own Strillio account, with everyone who will work in it; each user does it once on their own login. The agenda is dynamic: the prep agent builds it per client from the scope in the client brain (onboarding call, confirmed in the kick-off). Appointment setting only → blocks 1–6 and 8, ±60 min. Quotes from Strillio → block 7 is added and becomes a main part, ±90 min, with the salespeople present.
1.  Dashboard (10 min): what they see daily (new leads, called leads, appointments, follow-up speed, per source). Show a running example such as Dryplan, anonymised or with Dryplan's permission, since it is another client's data.
2.  Pipeline (10 min): their own steps as approved in the kick-off; when to move a lead (incl. voicemail 1, 2, …); a lost lead always gets a reason, which feeds the campaigns.
3.  Opportunity details (10 min): everything about one lead on one screen (form answers, source, notes, tasks, appointment). Open a lead live and move it through the steps.
4.  Communication overview (5 min): what the lead gets at which moment (mail, WhatsApp, sms, AI agent). The schedule itself is approved in the kick-off; here we only show where they see it and that it goes out.
5.  Inbox (5 min): all lead replies in one place per channel; replying from Strillio so the conversation stays with the lead.
6.  Test lead end to end (10 min): a test request through the real form → lead arrives, reaches the right salesperson, phone notification, first mail/WhatsApp goes out; book an appointment and check it lands in their calendar.
7.  Quotes in Strillio (±30 min, only if they quote from Strillio): their own quote template and products/prices set up in the prep; each salesperson makes a real quote live from an opportunity, sends it and sees it in the pipeline; follow-up of open quotes (e.g. reminder when a quote is open for 3 weeks), signed quote → won. Their example quote from the onboarding call is the basis for the template.
8.  Agreements and go (10 min): the 24-hour rule (weekdays only, at most 3 reminder mails per month in total, then patterns in the weekly update); other lead sources switched on the agreed date; their database imported and the start date of the reactivation flow (week 4–5); everyone can log in and has the app; who to contact with questions; launch date and first reporting meeting.
